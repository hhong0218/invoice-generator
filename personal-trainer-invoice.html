Personal Trainer Invoice Template

Personal trainers and fitness coaches bill in a mix of ways — single sessions, prepaid packages, and monthly coaching — often across both in-person and online clients. This guide shows how to structure a clear training invoice for each, and you can create one free in your browser.

Ways to bill personal training

Most training invoices use one of these structures, and many trainers use more than one depending on the client:

  • Per session. A set price per one-on-one session. Put the session rate in the unit price and the number of sessions in the quantity column — the simplest option for drop-in or pay-as-you-go clients.
  • Session packages. A block of sessions sold together, often at a per-session discount. List the package (for example “10-session package”) as one line and note the discount so the value is obvious.
  • Monthly coaching or retainer. A flat monthly fee covering a set number of sessions plus programming and check-ins. Bill it on a consistent date each month with the same description.
  • Online and hybrid coaching. Remote programming, app-based plans, and video check-ins — usually billed monthly, and easy to combine with in-person sessions on one invoice.

Packages, prepayment, and used sessions

Prepaid packages are the backbone of a stable training income, and clients like the per-session saving. When a client buys a package, invoice the full package amount up front as a single line. If you’d rather track usage, add a short note of the sessions remaining after this invoice so the client always knows where they stand. Be clear in your terms about expiry and cancellation — how long a package is valid, and your policy on late cancellations or no-shows — because those are the most common sources of billing disputes in training.

In-person, online, and add-ons

Many trainers now blend in-person sessions with online coaching, and one invoice can hold both: separate lines for “in-person session” and “online monthly programming” keep it clear. Extras like nutrition plans, custom workout programs, fitness assessments, or group sessions belong on their own lines too. If you travel to a client’s home or gym, a travel line is reasonable — just agree it in advance so it’s never a surprise.

Payment terms for trainers

Because so much training is prepaid, many trainers invoice before the sessions are delivered and expect payment on receipt or within a few days. For monthly coaching, invoice on the same date each cycle so clients can plan for it. Offer convenient payment methods — card, bank transfer, or a payment link — and state any late-cancellation fee on the invoice so the policy is visible in writing.

Look professional, get paid on time

A clean, branded invoice reinforces the trust a client places in you for their health and progress. Make sure each invoice has a unique number, the service dates or billing period, your business and the client’s details, itemized sessions or packages, any discount, the subtotal, tax if you charge it, and the total due. Frequently asked questions

Should I charge per session or sell packages?

Per-session billing suits drop-in and pay-as-you-go clients, while packages give you prepaid income and let you offer a per-session discount. Many trainers offer both and reserve packages for committed clients.

How do I invoice a prepaid package?

Invoice the full package amount up front as a single line, and optionally note the number of sessions remaining after this invoice. Spell out the package’s expiry and your cancellation policy in the terms to avoid disputes.

Can one invoice cover in-person and online coaching?

Yes. Use separate lines — for example “in-person session” and “online monthly programming” — so a hybrid arrangement stays clear on a single invoice.

What payment terms work for personal training?

Since training is often prepaid, many trainers invoice before delivery and expect payment on receipt or within a few days. For monthly coaching, bill on the same date each cycle and note any late-cancellation fee on the invoice.
