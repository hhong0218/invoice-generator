How to Send an Invoice by Email

Email is how most invoices reach a client, and how you send it quietly affects how fast you get paid. A clear subject line, a short professional message, and the invoice attached as a PDF make it effortless for the client to approve and pay. Here is exactly how to do it, with a template you can copy.

Attach a PDF, never an editable file

Always send the invoice as a PDF attachment, not a word-processor document or a link the client has to chase. A PDF looks the same on every device, can’t be accidentally altered, and reads as a finished, professional record. Name the file so it is easy to find later — something like Invoice-INV-0007-YourBusiness.pdf rather than document1.pdf. If you build the invoice with the generator, download the PDF and attach that file directly.

Write a clear subject line

The subject line decides whether your email is opened today or buried. Make it specific and scannable, including the word “invoice,” the number, and ideally the amount or due date:

  • Invoice INV-0007 from [Your Business] — $1,200 due July 1
  • Invoice #0007 for [Project] — due on receipt

A vague subject like “Following up” or “Hello” is easy to skip. A specific one signals exactly what the email is and what the client needs to do.

Keep the message short and professional

The body of the email should be three or four sentences: a greeting, what the invoice is for, the total and due date, and how to pay. Don’t bury the key facts — the client should be able to act without opening the attachment. Restating the amount and due date in the email itself is one of the simplest ways to get paid faster, because it removes a step.

Copy-paste email template

Subject: Invoice INV-0007 from [Your Business] — $1,200 due July 1 Hi [Client name], Please find attached invoice INV-0007 for [project or service], totaling $1,200. Payment is due by July 1, and you can pay by [bank transfer / card / payment link — details are on the invoice]. Thank you very much — please let me know if you have any questions. Best regards, [Your name] [Your business, phone]

Send it to the right person

An invoice sent to the wrong inbox sits unpaid. For a small client, that is usually your main contact; for a larger company, it is often an accounts-payable or billing address, sometimes with a required purchase order (PO) number on the invoice. Ask up front who invoices should go to and whether a PO is needed — getting this right the first time avoids the most common cause of delay. CC your own contact if billing goes to a separate department, so someone on the client side is expecting it.

Follow up on a schedule

Most overdue invoices are simply forgotten, not refused, so a calm follow-up routine recovers the majority of them. A few days before the due date, send a short friendly reminder; the day after it is due, reply to your original email (keeping the thread and the attachment intact) with a quick “just checking this didn’t slip through” note; a week or two overdue, send a firmer but still professional message referencing your terms. Replying on the original thread keeps the invoice attached and the history in one place.

Frequently asked questions

Should I send an invoice as a PDF or in the email body?

Attach it as a PDF, and also restate the key facts — total, due date, and how to pay — in the email body. The PDF is the formal record; the summary in the body lets the client act without opening the attachment, which speeds up payment.

What should the subject line of an invoice email say?

Be specific: include the word invoice, the invoice number, and ideally the amount or due date, for example “Invoice INV-0007 from [Your Business] — $1,200 due July 1.” A specific subject is far more likely to be opened and acted on than a vague one.

Who should I send the invoice to?

For small clients, your main contact; for larger companies, the accounts-payable or billing address, often with a required PO number on the invoice. Ask up front who invoices should go to and whether a PO is needed to avoid delays.

How do I follow up on an unpaid emailed invoice?

Reply on the original email thread so the invoice stays attached: a friendly reminder just before the due date, a short check-in the day after, and a firmer note referencing your terms if it runs a week or two overdue.
