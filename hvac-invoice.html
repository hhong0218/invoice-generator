HVAC Invoice Template

A heating and cooling job can involve a diagnostic fee, a flat-rate repair, refrigerant billed by the pound, and a maintenance plan renewal — sometimes all in one visit. An invoice that keeps those distinct gets approved faster and defends your pricing if the customer questions it later. You can build one free in your browser and download a print-ready PDF.

What an HVAC invoice should show

Whether the ticket is a no-cool call in July or a full furnace replacement, the same line-item structure works:

  • Diagnostic or service call fee. The flat charge for coming out and finding the fault. Many companies credit it toward the repair if the customer approves the work — if you do, show the credit as its own line.
  • Repair labor or flat-rate task. Either hours at your rate, or the flat-rate book price for the specific repair.
  • Parts. Capacitors, contactors, blower motors, igniters, thermostats — each significant part on its own line with a quantity and unit price.
  • Refrigerant. Billed by the pound with the type noted (for example R-410A), separate from labor.
  • Maintenance plan or tune-up. A seasonal tune-up or an annual agreement renewal, listed as its own service.
  • Equipment and installation. On replacements, the new unit and the install labor — plus any permit or disposal fee for the old equipment.

Flat-rate or time-and-materials?

Most residential HVAC work today is quoted flat-rate: the customer approves a fixed price for the repair before you start, and the invoice shows that task as a single line. Time-and-materials still suits unusual diagnostics and commercial accounts. Whichever you use, the invoice should match what was approved — if you quoted a flat rate, do not switch to itemized hours on the bill, because a mismatch between quote and invoice is the fastest way to trigger a dispute.

Billing refrigerant and parts

Refrigerant deserves its own line, priced per pound with the type stated. It is a regulated substance, prices vary by type, and customers increasingly ask about it — a clear line avoids the “why so expensive” call. For parts, list each significant component with a model or plain-language description (“45/5 dual run capacitor” reads better than “part”). Your parts pricing normally includes a markup over wholesale; that is standard and does not need to be broken out.

Maintenance agreements: your recurring revenue

Spring AC tune-ups and fall furnace checks are the recurring backbone of an HVAC business. Invoice agreements consistently — same description, same renewal date — and if members get a discounted repair rate, show the discount as a visible line so the customer sees the plan paying for itself. That single line does more to renew agreements than any reminder letter.

Deposits and system replacements

A full system replacement is a large ticket with equipment you must order, so a deposit up front is normal, with the balance due when the system is commissioned. Pass permit and inspection fees through at cost as their own lines. Frequently asked questions

Should I bill refrigerant separately from labor?

Yes. Price it per pound on its own line with the refrigerant type noted. It is a regulated, price-volatile material, and a clear line prevents the customer from assuming it was folded into an inflated labor charge.

Flat-rate or hourly — which should the invoice show?

Whichever the customer approved. If you quoted a flat-rate repair, invoice that task as one line at the approved price; do not restate it as hours and parts. The invoice should mirror the authorization exactly.

Should I take a deposit on a system replacement?

Yes — replacements involve equipment you order in advance, so a deposit at approval with the balance due at commissioning is standard practice. Show the deposit received as a credit line on the final invoice.

How do I invoice a diagnostic fee I waive on repair?

List the diagnostic fee as a line, then add a matching credit line such as “Diagnostic fee credited with repair.” The customer sees the value they received rather than the fee silently disappearing.
