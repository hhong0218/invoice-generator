Roofing Invoice Template

Roofing invoices carry more weight than most trades: the tickets are large, the work is staged around weather, and what is under the old shingles is unknown until tear-off. An invoice built for deposits, progress payments, and documented change orders protects both your cash and your reputation. You can create one free in your browser and download a print-ready PDF.

What a roofing invoice should show

A replacement, a repair, and a storm job all draw from the same set of line items:

  • Tear-off and disposal. Removing the existing roof, usually priced by the square (100 sq ft), with the dumpster or haul-away fee as its own line.
  • Materials by the square. Shingles or other roofing, underlayment, ice-and-water shield, drip edge, ridge cap — the major materials with quantities.
  • Flashing and penetrations. Chimney and wall flashing, pipe boots, and vents, listed so the detail work is visible.
  • Decking replacement. Rotten or delaminated sheathing replaced per sheet — the classic mid-job addition (more below).
  • Labor. Install labor, either folded into the per-square price or shown separately, matching how you quoted.
  • Permits and inspection. Passed through at cost as their own lines.

Deposits and progress payments

Few roofers should fund an entire roof out of pocket. The common structure is a deposit when the contract is signed (enough to cover the material order), sometimes a progress draw on larger or multi-phase jobs, and the balance on completion. Show every payment already received as a credit line on the final invoice so the arithmetic from contract price to balance due is visible at a glance — that one habit eliminates most “wait, what am I paying?” calls.

Change orders for what tear-off reveals

No one knows the condition of the decking until the old roof comes off. The professional move is to set a per-sheet price for decking replacement in the original contract, then document what you found — photos help — and confirm the added quantity with the customer before installing. On the invoice, that work appears as its own line referencing the change order (“Decking replacement per CO #1: 6 sheets”). Surprise charges with no paper trail are the single biggest source of roofing disputes; priced-in-advance change orders make the same charge routine.

Insurance claim work

Storm-damage jobs paid through insurance need the invoice to mirror the approved claim scope, because the carrier and the homeowner will both compare them. Keep line descriptions consistent with the claim paperwork, and bill any approved supplements as clearly separated additional lines rather than blending them into the original scope. If the homeowner owes a deductible, showing it explicitly keeps everyone honest about who pays what.

Warranty and the final invoice

The final invoice is a good place to record what the customer bought beyond the shingles: note your workmanship warranty term and the manufacturer warranty registered, and that cleanup — including the magnetic nail sweep — was completed. Frequently asked questions

How do I bill decking replacement found after tear-off?

Set a per-sheet price in the original contract, document the damage when you find it, get the customer’s go-ahead, and invoice it as its own change-order line with the quantity. Pre-priced and confirmed, it is a routine line instead of a disputed surprise.

Should I take a deposit on a roof replacement?

Yes — a deposit at contract signing that covers the material order is standard, with the balance due on completion. Some states cap deposit amounts for home improvement contracts, so check your local rules before setting a percentage.

How is an insurance claim job invoiced differently?

Match your line items to the approved claim scope, bill supplements as clearly separated lines, and show the homeowner’s deductible explicitly. The carrier and homeowner will compare the invoice against the claim, so consistency is everything.

Should the dumpster fee be its own line?

Yes. Disposal is a real, visible cost on every tear-off. Listing it separately keeps it from eating into your labor price and shows the customer where the money goes.
